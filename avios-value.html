What is an Avios worth to you?

Compare the cash you actually give up, find a break-even valuation and avoid paying more for points than you can use.

Published 12 November 2026 · Aviosmaxxing practical guide

On this page

  1. Compare the same purchase in cash first
  2. Estimate value from a trip you would actually take
  3. Keep a cash budget and an uncertainty margin
  4. Use this method in Aviosmaxxing
  5. Primary references to check before using real figures

An Avios balance is useful only when it changes what you can realistically book or buy. A large points total on its own cannot tell you whether a purchase route is good value. Start with the cash alternative you would actually choose, then work out what extra points cost. That keeps the decision tied to your budget and travel plans.

Worked examples: every price, reward quantity and valuation below is invented to explain the calculation. They are not current fares, card earning rates or programme offers.

Compare the same purchase in cash first

Suppose a retailer sells the item you want for £400. A different route costs £412 and offers an extra 2,000 Avios. Delivery, returns and the item itself are identical. The reward route asks you to pay £12 more now. Its break-even cost is:

£12 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 0.6p per extra Avios

If you can use those Avios at a personal value of 0.8p each, 2,000 are worth £16 to you. The £412 route then has an estimated net cost of £396, which is £4 better than £400. At a valuation of 0.5p, the points are worth £10 and the net cost is £402. The same offer is then £2 worse.

0.6p each£12£400
0.8p each£16£396

Now add a further detail: the £400 route also pays £8 cashback. Your real alternative is £392, not £400. Even the optimistic £396 estimate loses by £4. Include rewards on the alternative route before declaring an Avios offer a winner.

Estimate value from a trip you would actually take

For a possible redemption, subtract its cash charges from the comparable cash price, then divide by the Avios needed. Imagine a flight costs £230 in cash or 19,000 Avios plus £65. The headline calculation is (£230 − £65) ÷ 19,000 × 100, or about 0.87p per Avios.

That figure is useful only if the cash flight is a fair alternative. Compare the same journey, dates, baggage and flexibility. If you would otherwise choose a suitable £180 flight, the value against your own alternative becomes (£180 − £65) ÷ 19,000 × 100, about 0.61p. A more expensive fare you would never buy can exaggerate the apparent saving.

Also account for any differences you can price: extra positioning travel, an overnight stay, paid seats or rewards forgone on the cash fare. Use the amount you would genuinely pay for a benefit, rather than its advertised retail price.

Keep a cash budget and an uncertainty margin

The £412 purchase still requires £412 today. An estimated £16 of future travel value does not reduce the charge on your card. If the extra cash stretches your budget, the lower cash price can be the better choice even when the points calculation looks positive.

Use a conservative valuation when your travel dates are inflexible or you do not have a specific use for the points. A £1 or £2 estimated advantage can disappear with a small price change or a missing reward. Recalculate without an uncertain bonus and see whether the route still makes sense.

Use this method in Aviosmaxxing

  1. Record the cheapest suitable cash alternative, including its own rewards.
  2. Enter only verified earning and conversion rates in Tools → Calculator.
  3. Keep Avios and other programme points in separate columns until a current conversion is confirmed.
  4. Compare cash paid today as well as your estimated net cost.

The calculator estimates points; it does not establish a market price for them. Save the assumptions alongside your research so a later price or terms change is easy to spot. The stacking guide shows why a larger total can still lose to a cheaper checkout.

Primary references to check before using real figures

Use British Airways for your actual booking quote and applicable Avios terms, and the relevant issuer or programme for earning and conversion rules. This guide does not quote a live redemption price or an official fixed cash value for Avios.
