How to check whether rewards really stack

Separate card, shopping-portal and merchant rewards, check their earning bases, then compare the complete checkout.

Published 12 November 2026 · Aviosmaxxing practical guide

A purchase can involve several reward schemes, but their names appearing on the same screen does not prove they all pay together. Treat each proposed layer as a separate claim with its own eligible spend, conditions and evidence. Add the layers only after checking that they apply to your exact route.

Write down the three possible layers

Payment rewards depend on the card or account used and its current terms. Shopping-portal rewards depend on the merchant offer and the route into the checkout. Merchant loyalty rewards depend on the retailer's own membership and transaction rules. These can have different earning bases and can use different points currencies.

For every layer, record the programme name, rate, qualifying amount, cap and exclusions. Check whether activation, a particular payment method or a specific booking channel is required. Do not interpret the absence of an obvious warning as permission to stack; find the terms that make the combination eligible.

Illustrative example: all rates, prices and conditions in the following comparison are invented. They do not describe an actual merchant or card.

A 680-point checkout can lose to a 195-point checkout

Imagine a portal route costs £200. A fictional card pays one Avios per eligible pound, so it earns 200 Avios. The portal pays three Avios per pound but, under this invented offer's terms, only £160 qualifies. Its reward is 480 Avios. The combined total is 680 Avios, assuming both layers have been confirmed eligible.

A direct route costs £195 and earns 195 Avios from the same fictional card, with no portal reward. At a personal valuation of 0.8p per Avios:

The direct route is £1.12 better on these assumptions. The portal route buys 485 extra Avios for £5, a break-even cost of about 1.03p each. Counting the portal's 480 Avios against a zero-reward alternative would miss the 195 Avios available on the cheaper route.

Do not reuse the same spend figure automatically

A checkout total, card-eligible amount and portal-eligible amount are separate inputs. Read the actual offer to establish how delivery, taxes, discounts and other charges are treated. The £160 base in the example is a fictional term, not a general rule about excluding a particular charge.

Programme points also need their own column. If a card pays bank points and a portal pays Avios, report both balances separately until you have checked the current conversion and whether you can use it. The word “points” is not a shared unit of measurement.

Check the route before paying

  • Open the merchant-specific offer and read its exclusions, not just the headline rate.
  • Check whether your item category, customer status, promotional code and payment route qualify.
  • Follow the portal's own tracking instructions. If a browser setting affects the required tracking, decide whether you want to change it rather than assuming the reward will appear.
  • Compare the final basket with a direct checkout, including delivery, returns and cancellation rights.
  • Keep dated evidence of the offer and your order for any later query, without sharing card numbers or passwords.

Separate an estimate from a confirmed reward

Recording a click or seeing a pending entry is not the same as receiving a usable reward. Use the programme's published process and timescales for a missing or declined transaction. Returns and changes should prompt a fresh check of the applicable terms. Keep uncertain rewards out of a cash budget.

In Aviosmaxxing, the calculator asks for card and portal confirmations separately. That reflects the two different claims. If either remains unclear, compare a conservative route without that layer as well as the optimistic total. Read the verification checklist before treating an AI-generated stacking suggestion as a purchase instruction.

Primary references for your own calculation

Use the exact merchant's offer inside the portal you intend to use, plus your issuer's product terms. Starting points include American Express UK cards and Barclaycard Avios cards. No current earning rate or universal stacking permission is asserted here.
